# User journeys

Status — interaction requirements. G2 supplies the accepted access contract. The customer entry journey is a G3 acceptance specification for [#163](https://github.com/Maeximilion/MaexSocial/issues/163).

The first user should understand what MaexSocial will do, for which account, when, and with which authority. The first slice follows brand setup → connected account → content draft → review → schedule/publication → result.

## Access and dashboard journeys

The public customer entry offers sign-in and self-signup. The dashboard shows the active workspace name, customer role and permitted actions. The server checks active membership and permissions for every action. The two customer roles are Employee and Manager. An active Manager with fresh enrolled MFA can invite or remove an Employee in their own workspace. Only DEV/Admin can grant Manager access, through the separate signed Windows client and private, device-bound administration path. That path shows a minimal customer directory and an explicit target before management actions. Customer signup, invitations, recovery and the portal never grant platform authority. [Accepted G2 decision](https://github.com/Maeximilion/MaexSocial/issues/15#issuecomment-6681723454) · [Access design](17_ACCESS_SECURITY.md)

### Customer entry acceptance journey

The fixed customer destination after successful authentication and membership selection is `/customer` on the configured HTTPS customer origin. A sign-in that is part of an invitation resumes only the server-bound invitation flow before workspace selection. A signed-out customer returns to the public customer entry. Use configured, fixed destinations for these transitions. Ignore caller-supplied return URLs and reauthorize membership on arrival. The staging entry URL and identity callback URL must be recorded under #163 after the host is approved.

Complete each row with Tab, Shift+Tab and the native activation keys. Check logical order, visible focus, a working skip link where navigation repeats, and programmatic names for controls and the current workspace. With a screen reader, confirm the page title, heading, workspace and role, and that new errors and results are announced. For an invalid field, focus the error summary linked to the field or the first invalid control with its associated error. For a page-level failure, focus the error heading or summary, then make the safe next action reachable. Preserve safe input when retrying and keep passwords and codes out of redisplayed form values. Repeat at a narrow viewport and with browser zoom to check that messages, controls and focus stay visible.

| Step | Visible outcome and keyboard focus | Permitted next destination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public entry | Start in normal document order. Tab reaches labeled sign-in, self-signup and public notice links. Opening a form puts focus at its heading or first labeled field. | Customer sign-in, signup or a public notice on an approved surface. |
| Sign in | A successful sign-in leads to workspace selection when needed. An unsuccessful sign-in uses a generic message that does not distinguish missing, pending, suspended or incorrect credentials. Focus the message and expose retry and recovery controls. | Active membership selection or `/customer` with an active membership. On failure, stay in sign-in or go to recovery. |
| Self-signup and verify | For new, existing and pending emails, focus the same public confirmation with mailbox instructions. The mailbox owner explicitly verifies, then sets fresh initial credentials after any unverified credentials, factors and sessions are invalidated. Successful activation creates exactly one new workspace with Employee access. Focus the completion heading, with sign-in as the next action. A verified identity receives sign-in or recovery guidance privately and keeps its credentials and memberships. | Sign in and enter the newly activated workspace or select an existing authorized workspace after ordinary sign-in. Email alone grants no access to an existing workspace. |
| Invitation for a verified identity | Opening the link makes no change. Require sign-in as the matching identity. Show the intended workspace and customer role on the confirmation screen and focus its heading. Collect any missing required names. Confirm explicitly and preserve existing credentials, profile and memberships. | After acceptance, enter the invited workspace via `/customer`. Declining or leaving the flow grants nothing. |
| Invitation for a new or unverified identity | Require mailbox proof and discard any unverified credentials, factors, challenges and sessions before setup. Show the intended workspace and role. Use labeled fields for first name, last name and the invitee's own password. Indicate that other profile fields are optional. Activate the authorized membership once after explicit confirmation and recheck current issuer authority and tenant status. | After acceptance and authentication, enter the invited workspace via `/customer`. On validation failure, stay in the form with field feedback. |
| Wrong account or invalid link | Focus a clear error with a next action. For a mismatching signed-in identity, offer to sign out and use the intended account without revealing the invited address or restricted workspace details. For an expired, replaced, used or revoked link, explain that the link cannot be used without revealing account or grant status. | Sign out and authenticate as the intended account, restart verification or recovery from the public entry, or ask the invitation issuer for a new invitation as appropriate. No grant is made. |
| Choose or switch workspace | Show only active authorized memberships. Focus the chooser heading on arrival. Select by keyboard and submit an explicit choice. After success, move focus to the workspace heading or main region and announce the workspace and role. Focus the error after a denied switch or if there are no active memberships. | `/customer` in the selected workspace under a rotated session for an in-session switch. After denial, offer selection if the account still has active access, or sign-out if it has none. No other workspace's content is shown. |
| Switch account | Sign out of the current customer session, land on the signed-out confirmation, then use customer sign-in to identify the intended account. Verify that an existing identity-provider session cannot silently return the person to the previous account when they choose a different account. | Public entry and sign-in, then only the new identity's authorized workspaces. Workspace selection changes the active tenant within one account. |
| Expired session or denied access | For a missing or expired session, focus a sign-in prompt. For a signed-in user whose membership or target is denied, focus a generic access message that exposes no denied workspace, object or role details. | Public sign-in for a lost session. For a denied target, return to `/customer` only with a still-authorized workspace or offer authorized workspace selection or sign-out. |
| Recover access | Focus an equivalent public confirmation after a reset or resend request. Guide the mailbox owner through explicit recovery and announced results. Reset revokes prior sessions. Recovery does not restore suspended or removed access. Sensitive Manager actions require restored enrollment and fresh MFA. | Fresh sign-in after successful recovery. An unusable link leads back to the public recovery entry. |
| Sign out | Activate the labeled sign-out control. Confirm the session is revoked. Focus and announce the signed-out heading, with sign-in reachable by keyboard. Use Back, reload and a direct protected URL to verify that each attempt exposes no private payload. | Fixed public customer entry. A fresh sign-in is required before returning to a protected workspace. |

Legal/operator information and the privacy notice must be reachable without signing in from public entry, sign-in, signup, invitation and recovery screens, and from the signed-out confirmation. Keep those links keyboard-accessible and usable without carrying invite or recovery tokens into the notice URL or referrer. ### Local portal evidence and open staging checks

Source baseline `fbf4d2cc979ec0fb094438f80e372e230bc34241`. The local customer fixture accepts `https://customer:8443`. `GET /` is a signed-out placeholder. `GET /customer` requires a valid customer session and shows the workspace, role and active workspace selector, with links to `/customer/drafts` and `/customer/facts`. `POST /customer/switch` rotates the tenant-bound session and responds `303 /customer`. `POST /customer/logout` revokes the session, clears its cookies and responds `303 /` in the dashboard fixture. A denied protected request returns a generic response, with no accessible recovery or sign-in path in that response. The separate private OIDC and logout fixtures exercise identity components in isolation. ## Required visible states

Maxi's [media-library and calendar request](18_MEDIA_LIBRARY.md) adds a proposed journey: private image/video upload → AI-assisted creation → exact-variant review → calendar/recurrence → per-channel publication result. Optional Campaigns shows separate audience, dates, ad budget and activation approval. Media library, Create, Calendar and Campaigns are proposed dashboard areas, not implemented screens or accepted G2 layout.

- Account connection shows platform, account identity, granted capabilities and expired/revoked access.
- Draft review shows source facts, image/media provenance, exact revision and intended destinations.
- Publication shows scheduled, submitting, confirmed, failed and outcome-unknown states. A timeout must not display a false successful publication.
- Autonomy controls show active policy, account scope, frequency, budget and stop state.
- Activity history links each action to revision, actor/policy and external post or job identity.
- Media jobs show queued/running/completed/failed/cancellation state and actual versus reserved cost where supported.

## Acceptance questions

Can a user distinguish a draft from a live post? Can they identify the account before publication? Can they stop future activity and see what is already in flight? Can keyboard and screen-reader users complete the primary journey? Can a failed provider call be understood and recovered without duplicate publication?

Can customers reach only their own permitted data? Does an administrator always see which customer an action affects? Do login, expiry and recovery provide accessible paths without disclosing account existence? The ACCESS acceptance cases in the [security design](17_ACCESS_SECURITY.md) cover these boundaries through browser and server tests.

MS-008 chooses the first journey and produces an accessible prototype. MS-010 verifies it end to end with synthetic content and an authorized test account.
